2 Time value of money (additivity of value)

FV = C0 × (1 + r)^T PV = CT / (1 + r)^T NPV = PV of inflows − PV of costs PV = Σ Ct / (1 + r)^t (t = 0..T) value is additive; PV and FV give the same decision Effective annual rate: EAR = (1 + r/m)^m − 1 Compounding m times a year: FV = C0 × (1 + r/m)^(m×T) Continuous compounding: FV = C0 × e^(rT) Perpetuity: PV = C / r Growing perpetuity: PV = C / (r − g) Annuity: PV = C × [1/r − 1/(r(1+r)^T)] = C × a(T, r) Geometric series: a(1 − q^(n+1)) / (1 − q); if q < 1 and n → ∞: a / (1 − q) Annuity due: PV0 = C + C × a(r, T−1) Deferred annuity: discount twice
TrapUnless stated otherwise, cash flows occur at the end of the period (date 0 = now). "4% compounded quarterly": 4% is the stated rate, quarterly rate = 4%/4 = 1%, EAR = 1.01^4 − 1 = 4.06%. "4% compounded yearly": 4% is the EAR. The EAR is the rate that really matters.

3 Cash is king (financial statements and cash flow)

Balance sheet: Assets = Liabilities + Shareholder's Equity NWC = short-term assets − short-term liabilities NWC = Shareholder's Equity + Long-Term Debt − Fixed Assets Income statement: Revenue − Expenses = Income Operating cash flow = EBIT − Taxes + Depreciation FCF = Operating cash flow − Capital spending − Addition to NWC (cash free for investors) Cash identity: CF(A) = CF(B) + CF(S) CF to creditors CF(B) = interest paid − net new borrowing (change in long-term debt) CF to shareholders CF(S) = dividends + share repurchases − new equity issued Operating cycle = inventory period + accounts receivable period Cash cycle = Operating cycle − Accounts payable period (periods in days: days in inventory, receivables, payables)
DefBook (accounting) value = historical cost adjusted for depreciation. Market value = price at which willing buyers and sellers trade. Much of a firm's true value is not on the balance sheet (management, proprietary assets...). Depreciation and deferred taxes are noncash items; income is not cash flow.
DefOperating working capital (OWC) = short-term operating assets − short-term operating liabilities. Excess cash is a financing or strategic item, not operating. The need for OWC comes from unsynchronised flows: you pay suppliers before customers pay you.

4-5 NPV and investment rules

NPV = PV(inflows) − PV(outflows) = Σ CFt / (1 + r)^t Operating CF = EBIT − taxes + Depr = EBIT(1−τ) + Depr = Revenue(1−τ) − Cost(1−τ) + Depr × τ (Depr × τ = depreciation tax shield) Total CF = Operating CF − Capital spending − Investment in NWC After-tax salvage = sale price − τ × (sale price − book value) Fisher: (1 + r_nom) = (1 + r_real) × (1 + π) Payback = number of years to recover the initial cost IRR: rate such that NPV = 0 Check: cash flows −200, 50, 100, 150 → IRR ≈ 19.44%
Rule NPVAccept if NPV > 0. Discount rate = required return = cost of capital = opportunity cost of funds. Positive NPV raises firm value (value additivity). In efficient markets NPV = 0 (price = fundamental value); NPV = 0 is not a bad deal.
ChoosingUnlimited funds and no mutually exclusive projects: take all NPV > 0. Budget constraint or mutually exclusive projects: take the feasible combination with the maximal sum of NPVs.
Cash flowsUse incremental after-tax cash flows. Exclude sunk costs and accounting earnings. Include opportunity costs, side effects (synergy, erosion) and incremental costs. Debt effect (interest) is left out: assume debt is independent of the project. NWC investment comes from higher inventory, cash buffer, sales on credit, lower payables.
InflationDiscount nominal cash flows at the nominal rate, real cash flows at the real rate: same NPV.
PaybackAccept if payback ≤ cutoff. Weak: ignores time value, ignores cash flows after the cutoff, biased against long-term projects, arbitrary cutoff, may accept NPV < 0. Strong: simple, biased toward liquidity. Discounted payback uses discounted cash flows.
IRRAccept if IRR > required return; among alternatives, highest IRR. If C0 < 0 and all later flows > 0: IRR > r ⇔ NPV > 0. IRR is the x-axis intercept of the NPV profile. NPV gives a dollar value, IRR a rate.
IRR trapsMultiple IRRs: K sign changes → up to K IRRs (e.g. −200, 200, 800, −800 → 0% and 100%): do not use the IRR. Scale problem: A (−1, +2) IRR 100% vs B (−10, +15) IRR 50%: compare NPVs or the incremental IRR (IRR of B − A). Timing problem: A (−10,000; 10,000; 1,000; 1,000) IRR 16.04% vs B (−10,000; 1,000; 1,000; 12,000) IRR 12.94%; NPV profiles cross at 10.55% (below it B is better, above it A). IRR assumes reinvestment at the IRR; use NPV or the incremental IRR.
Why NPVUses all cash flows, discounts them properly, assumes reinvestment at the discount rate.
